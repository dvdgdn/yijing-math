# Supplied Context: Reported Phenomena and Competing Explanations

An earlier investigation in this program reported the phenomena below about the received (King Wen) order. **None has been independently verified.** A separate verification is under way. The reported p-values are deliberately withheld, so that your analysis plan is not anchored to them.

## Conventions

A hexagram is a word x = (x₁, …, x₆) in {0, 1}⁶, bottom (x₁) to top (x₆), yang = 1. R reverses the word; C complements every line. The 32 consecutive pairs {2k−1, 2k} of the received order are reported to be each either {x, Rx} (28 pairs) or, for the 8 hexagrams with Rx = x, {x, Cx} (4 pairs). The upper canon is hexagrams 1–30, the lower canon 31–64. A "figure" is an orbit of the group generated by R. The nuclear map is N(x) = (x₂, x₃, x₄, x₃, x₄, x₅).

You may use other representations, but state how your analysis relates to these conventions.

## Reported phenomena

- **P1 (C-0003).** Each canon contains exactly 18 figures.
- **P2 (C-0006).** The doubled-trigram hexagrams in the upper canon (1, 2, 29, 30) are exactly those whose trigram is unchanged by reversal; those in the lower canon (51, 52, 57, 58) are those whose trigram is changed by reversal.
- **P3 (C-0009).** The first and last pairs, {1, 2} and {63, 64}, are exactly the four words {000000, 111111, 101010, 010101}. Every hexagram reaches this set within two applications of N.

## Competing explanations reported or raised

For P3 (dispute DSP-001):
- (a) the end positions reflect the attractor structure of the nuclear map;
- (b) the end positions reflect line-position extremes: the four words are the constant words and the words with all lines in correct position or all incorrect (C-0012);
- (c) chance or unmodeled history;
- (d) other criteria not yet considered.

For the order as a whole (dispute DSP-002):
- H2 (C-0024): the order carries structure from the symmetry group generated by R and C beyond the pairing itself;
- H0 (C-0025): beyond the pairing, the order is indistinguishable from random arrangements of the pairs with respect to natural statistics.
