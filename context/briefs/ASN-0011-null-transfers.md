# Conditional ensembles: transfer scope

The supplied source statements have not been independently verified here. They concern exact uniform sampling of nonnegative integer matrices with fixed margins, and approximate counting with cell upper bounds. They do not automatically describe a uniform law on sequences or on labelled objects. The unbounded sampler has no forbidden cells. The bounded counting theorem is not itself a delivered bounded sampler.

A finite pilot used row and column margins (2,2). The possible tables [[a,2−a],[2−a,a]], a=0,1,2, have masses (1/3,1/3,1/3) under uniform tables. Uniform allocation of two labelled objects of each of two types to two rows of size two induces multiplicities choose(2,a)choose(2,2−a)=(1,4,1), giving (1/6,2/3,1/6). An exact sampler for one law is wrong for the other.

Another pilot allows only reversal or complement edges between all 64 six-bit words. Its eight two-vertex components and twelve four-cycles yield 4,096 perfect matchings, enumerable exactly. General entropy/counting machinery adds little there. Adding arbitrary order constraints need not leave a matching, table or matroid problem.

Keep a small fully enumerated reference problem, establish the correspondence between elementary outcomes and table/graph states, and quantify induced multiplicities. Compare a uniform-state law with at least one justified alternative. If the imported result is unnecessary, demonstrate that rather than implementing it for its own sake.
