# Source Material: Divination Procedures and Changing Lines

This note describes source material. It makes no claim about what is mathematically significant. Read it together with the description of lines, trigrams, and hexagrams.

## Line values

In the received divinatory tradition, a hexagram is built by generating six lines one at a time, from line 1 (bottom) to line 6 (top). Each generated line takes one of four values, conventionally numbered 6, 7, 8 and 9:

| Value | Traditional name | Drawn as | Changes? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | old yin (老陰) | broken | yes, becomes unbroken |
| 7 | young yang (少陽) | unbroken | no |
| 8 | young yin (少陰) | broken | no |
| 9 | old yang (老陽) | unbroken | yes, becomes broken |

Lines with values 6 or 9 are called changing or moving lines. The six generated values determine a primary hexagram (本卦). If any lines are changing, changing them gives a second hexagram (之卦).

## Procedures

- The *Xici zhuan* (繫辭傳, "Commentary on the Appended Statements", one of the "Ten Wings") describes a yarrow-stalk procedure, commonly called the *dayan* (大衍) method.
- Later practice also uses a method with three coins.

The details of these procedures and their interpretation differ between sources and commentators.

## Reading rules

Traditions give rules for which texts to consult when several lines change: the judgment of the primary or second hexagram, or particular line statements. These rules differ between traditions; one widely cited set is attributed to Zhu Xi (朱熹).
