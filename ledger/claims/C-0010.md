## Statement

Under the pair-shuffling null model (shuffling the 32 pairs and their orientations), the probability that both pairs contained in A ({1, 2} and {63, 64}) land at the two ends of the sequence is 0.0021. Conditional on Qian/Kun opening the sequence, the probability is 1/31.

## Definitions and assumptions

DEF-013. Assumes the event is defined as "the two A-pairs occupy positions 1 and 32 among the pairs, in either order"; the source does not say whether orientation within the end pairs matters.

## Provenance

Reported values only.

## Scope and limitations

One null model. The event was selected after the pattern was noticed, so the probability is descriptive rather than a test result (see `methods/standards.md` §7).

## Objections and alternatives

- INV-0001 itself identifies the null model as its weakest assumption: if the order accreted over time or was subject to unmodeled constraints, the p-values are miscalibrated.
- Post hoc selection and multiple comparisons: the endpoint fact was noticed among many possible patterns.
- Registrar note (not verified): the reported 0.0021 is close to 2/(32·31) ≈ 0.00202, which suggests a Monte Carlo estimate of a quantity that is exactly computable; the verifier should compute it exactly under a precisely stated model.
- Explanation of the placement is disputed (DSP-001).

## Further investigation

Exact computation and sensitivity to alternative null models (ASN-0002); pre-registered comparison of explanations (ASN-0005).
