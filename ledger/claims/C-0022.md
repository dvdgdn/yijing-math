## Statement

The pre-Han layers of the tradition (e.g. the received pairing and the nuclear relation) are R-equivariant, while Han constructions (the Jing Fang palaces; the Mawangdui order) break R down to the trigram split (l, u).

Prediction stated in the source: pair structure appears in every early ordering that respects R; if the Chu bamboo manuscript, wherever its order is recoverable, breaks pairs, the conjecture fails.

## Definitions and assumptions

Mathematical part: the R-equivariance or non-equivariance of specific structures (C-0007, C-0019, C-0021, C-0023). Historical part: assignment of each structure to a "pre-Han" or "Han" layer. That assignment is not argued in the source.

## Scope and limitations

Rests on historical dating assumptions as much as on mathematics. INV-0001 calls it "a pattern I noticed" and proposes a pre-registered test (Q-002).

## Objections and alternatives

- The dating of the nuclear-hexagram concept, and of the received order itself, are historical assumptions; if either is later than assumed, the stratification changes.
- A Han manuscript (Mawangdui) may preserve an earlier ordering tradition, so "found in a Han source" and "Han construction" are not the same.
- With few structures examined, a two-way split could arise by chance.

## Further investigation

A pre-registered classification of attested orderings and procedures by symmetry (Q-002, DIR-004), plus historical checks of dating and of the Chu manuscript (Q-001).
